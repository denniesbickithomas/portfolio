Know the mailroom line before you walk over

At ASU's Hassayampa Academic Village, mailroom waits range from 0 to more than 30 minutes, and residents have to walk over just to find out. Our team designed a resident app that uses live camera footage to show how many people are in line and the estimated wait, without ever showing anyone the video.

When
Fall 2025
Team
5 MSBA students (Team 13)
Users
300+ first-year residents
Tech
Python · YOLOv8n · OpenCV · Colab
The solution How it works Validation Roadmap My role

The solution

Some students spend more than an hour walking to the mailroom and waiting. The app answers one question, "is it worth going now?", and adds what residents need once they decide to go:

  • Live queue length and estimated wait
  • Mailroom hours and package details
  • A way to message mailroom staff
  • Privacy by design: users see counts and estimates, never video

How it works

  1. YOLOv8 detects people in each video frame.
  2. Only people whose feet fall inside a polygon drawn around the queue area are counted, so passers-by are ignored.
  3. Estimated wait = (people in queue + 1) × 2-minute average service time ÷ 2 servers. These queuing assumptions came from mailroom staff.
  4. The prototype outputs an annotated video with queue length and wait time on every frame.

Validation

Small proof-of-concept sample

The prototype counted the queue correctly in 7 of 7 sample frames (mean absolute error of 0). That's promising but small, and the plan is to validate again with a month of real data once cameras are installed.

Roadmap

MVP (Jan–Feb 2026) → one month of queue data to refine and validate → beta testing → fall 2026 launch, then expansion to dining halls and other mailrooms. A later version would forecast waits with a regression model on time of day, day of week and season.

My role

  • Owned and presented "The Solution": the app concept, the feature set and the resident use cases.
  • Teammates built most of the computer-vision model and code.
