Cutting sales-proposal cycle time

Proposals at "Gentech" took so long that they piled up and sales were lost. We analyzed 250,000 records of the 9-step ERP proposal process, found the two steps that cause most of the delay, and designed fixes plus a control plan to keep the gains.

Define

The project charter set the goal: cut proposal cycle time by 25–30%. We mapped the current process as a swim-lane diagram with 7 lanes and 19 steps, covering Seller, Bid Support, Proposal Support Manager, Product Design, Selling Team, Brand Manager and Client. The map showed 5 revision loops that add no value.

Measure

Average total cycle time was about 32 days. Against a 55-day service-level agreement there were 5,418 defects in 250,000 proposals, which gives 21,672 defects per million opportunities, a sigma level of 2.02.

Analyze

  • Segmentation: geography drives the variation, not brand. North America was consistently the slowest region.
  • Bottlenecks: two steps, configuration review and pricing/brand approval, take about 70% of total cycle time.
  • Complexity: bid complexity and cycle time are 83.6% correlated, and the relationship is logarithmic.
  • Root causes: we broke the process into eight time deltas and sorted the causes into a fishbone diagram under People, Process, Technology and Information. Causes included routing errors, manual re-entry, ERP lag, missing seller information, and every bid going through the brand manager.
  • Stability: X-bar and R control charts show the process is out of control. In the FMEA, the highest risk priority number (210) was for the range exceeding the upper control limit.

Improve & control

  • Standard templates for the configuration-review step.
  • Dedicated review and approval cells, so not every bid waits for the brand manager.
  • SLA enforcement in North America, one shared communication platform, and cross-training.
  • A control plan with monthly control charts, automated alerts, named step owners and a regular audit schedule.
Projected impact (not measured)

A roughly 30% efficiency gain on the two bottleneck steps saves about 6–7 days, bringing the forecast cycle time to 24–26 days, down from about 32. That's a 19–25% reduction, close to the charter's 25–30% goal.
