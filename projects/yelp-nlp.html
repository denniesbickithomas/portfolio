Can Yelp reviews predict restaurant success?

We asked whether the words customers write can tell us which restaurants will close. The answer was mostly "no," and explaining why turned out to be the useful finding for managers.

When
Fall 2025
Team
5 MSBA students (Group 1)
Data
Yelp Open Dataset · 72,124 restaurant reviews
Tools
Python · VADER · scikit-learn · seaborn
Approach Results The real insight

Approach

We merged 100,000 reviews with 9,973 businesses (average 3.58 stars, about 26% closed), filtered to restaurants, then:

  1. Scored the sentiment of every review with VADER.
  2. Converted the text into 52,913 TF-IDF word features.
  3. Trained a logistic regression to predict open vs. closed, using an 80/20 train-test split.

Results

  • Accuracy: 74.65%, with 98% recall for open restaurants but only 12% for closed ones.
  • Words tied to staying open: amazing, delicious, perfect, friendly, recommend.
  • Words tied to closing: disappointed, rude, dirty, manager, overpriced, bland.

The real insight

74.65% sounds good until you see the baseline.

About 73% of the test set was "open," so a model that always says "open" would score about 73%. Review text on its own is a weak predictor of closure. The value of the project was the diagnosis: class imbalance hides the cases that matter, and closure probably shows up in trends rather than in individual reviews.

Next steps we proposed: SMOTE resampling, plus features for star-rating trends, review-volume trends and business attributes.
