Two big bets for an EV maker

GreenFleet Motors faced two decisions under real uncertainty: whether to pay for a survey before buying a lithium site, and how large to build a new EV plant. We used decision trees and Monte Carlo simulation to give management a clear answer and a clear sense of the risk.

When
Fall 2025
Focus
Decision trees · Monte Carlo simulation
Type
Business case
Tools

Lithium site Plant capacity

Decision 1: buy the survey, invest now, or walk away?

A $300K geological survey could tell GreenFleet whether a lithium site was Rich (40% prior, +$4M), Moderate (35%, $0) or Dry (25%, −$2.5M). We built a decision tree with Bayesian-revised probabilities to price what the survey would really be worth.

$975K
expected value of the best choice: invest now
$228K
value of the survey's information (EVSI), below its $300K cost
$625K
value of perfect information (EVPI), the most any survey could be worth

Recommendation: invest now without the survey. A tornado sensitivity analysis showed that the probability the site is Rich drives the decision. If that probability falls below 35%, the survey is worth reconsidering, which gives management a trigger to watch rather than a one-off answer.

Decision 2: how big a plant?

We ran a 5-year Monte Carlo simulation where demand is correlated across years (correlation = 0.4ⁿ for years n apart), so a strong year tends to be followed by another strong year.

25,000
units: recommended capacity
≈ $685M
mean 5-year profit (median ≈ $914M)
≈ −$392M
worst simulated case

Recommendation: build for 25,000 units. Above that, the downside risk rises sharply, so we advised a hard cap of 30,000.
